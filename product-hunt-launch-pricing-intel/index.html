Product Hunt Launch Pricing & Website Intel reads Product Hunt leaderboards and goes one step further than a launch list: for every launch it follows the Product Hunt link to the product's real website, finds the pricing page and extracts the plans, prices, billing periods, free tier or trial, and the lowest paid price. You get clean, structured records you can export as JSON, CSV or Excel, call through the Apify API, or plug into n8n, Make and AI agents through MCP.

Pick a period, set a limit and run. With no dates it reads the latest finished day.

Why use Product Hunt Launch Pricing & Website Intel?

  • Competitor and market research. See what every new product in your category charges, whether it has a free tier, and how it positions its plans, without opening hundreds of pages.
  • Lead lists for B2B sales. Each row has the product website and its domain, so you can feed fresh launches into enrichment and outreach, and filter by topic, votes or pricing model first.
  • Pricing benchmarks. Collect lowest paid price and pricing model (freemium, paid, free, usage based, contact sales) across a week, a month or a year of launches.
  • Launch monitoring. Schedule a daily run with the "only new" option and receive each launch once.

Output fields

Each record contains the launch (rank, productName, tagline, topics, votes, comments, featuredAt, productHuntUrl), the website (websiteUrl, websiteDomain, websiteStatus, websiteTitle) and the pricing (pricingStatus, pricingPageUrl, pricingModel, hasFreeTier, hasFreeTrial, contactSales, currency, lowestPaidPrice, lowestPaidPeriod, pricingConfidence, plans).

When a run finds nothing you get one row with found: false (not billed) and the run status explains why.

How reliable is the pricing data?

Pricing pages have no common format, so prices are read from the visible text of the page. pricingConfidence tells you how clear the page was: high, medium or low. Treat low as a pointer to the pricing page, not as a final price. Pages that render prices only with JavaScript, hide them behind a login or block automated requests return pricingStatus other than parsed, and the website URL stays in the row.

Product Hunt page coverage

A leaderboard page lists the launches Product Hunt shows on its first page, about 15 to 20 per day, week, month or year. To cover more days, pass several dates.

Is it legal to scrape this data?

The Actor reads only publicly available pages and does not log in or bypass access controls. You are responsible for using the data in line with applicable laws

(including data protection rules) and the source's terms. If you find an issue, open it in the Issues tab; problems are answered within one business day.

Call it from your code

Run the Actor and get the results in one request. Replace YOUR_APIFY_TOKEN with the token from your Apify account settings.

curl -X POST "https://api.apify.com/v2/acts/datagrit~product-hunt-launch-pricing-intel/run-sync-get-dataset-items?token=YOUR_APIFY_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"period":"daily","fetchPricing":true,"maxItems":20}'
import { ApifyClient } from 'apify-client';

const client = new ApifyClient({ token: process.env.APIFY_TOKEN });
const run = await client.actor('datagrit/product-hunt-launch-pricing-intel').call({
  "period": "daily",
  "fetchPricing": true,
  "maxItems": 20
});
const { items } = await client.dataset(run.defaultDatasetId).listItems();
console.log(items.length, items[0]);

Frequently asked questions

How fresh is the data?

Every run reads Product Hunt and the product websites live.

Can I schedule runs?

Yes, use Apify schedules with the "only new" option to get each launch once.

Why is pricingStatus not "parsed" for some launches?

The website may be unreachable, may block automated requests, or may show prices only after a click. The row still has the website and the reason.

Something looks wrong.

Open an issue with the input you used; layout changes at the source are fixed quickly.
