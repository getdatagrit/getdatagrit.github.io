How to measure Telegram channel reach and growth without the Telegram API

Get subscribers, view rate, posting cadence, reactions and growth since the last run for public Telegram channels, from Python or cURL, no login.

Published 2026-10-06 · uses the Telegram Channel Analytics: Growth and Reach Actor

A Telegram channel with three million subscribers can deliver fewer views per post than a channel with fifteen thousand. Anyone who buys ads in channels, picks partners for cross-promotion, or watches competitors learns this quickly: the subscriber count says little, and the number that matters is how many subscribers actually open a post.

On 6 October 2026 a news channel with 3.04M subscribers had a median of 139,000 views per post, a view rate of 4.57%. A small finance channel with 15.1K subscribers had a median of 1,045 views and a view rate of 6.92%. The smaller channel reaches a larger share of its audience, and that is the comparison an ad buyer needs.

This post explains where those numbers come from, what a homemade script gets wrong, and how to collect them for a list of channels through the Apify API.

Where the data comes from

Telegram serves a public web preview of every channel that has it enabled, at t.me/s/<username>. It needs no login and shows the channel header (title, description, subscriber count, counters for photos, videos and links) and about 20 of the latest posts with their date, view count, reactions and forwarding information. Older posts are reached with a ?before=<id> parameter.

That is enough for a useful audit, but not for exact accounting. The pitfalls are worth knowing before you write your own parser:

  • Large numbers are rounded. The header shows 10.5M or 55.9K, not an exact count. A subscriber change computed between two rounded values is an estimate. The Telegram Channel Analytics Actor marks this with subscribersApproximate and subscribersChangeApproximate; for small channels the count is exact.
  • Fresh posts have unfinished view counts. A post published an hour ago will collect most of its views in the next day. The Actor builds the view sample from posts older than 24 hours when at least five exist.
  • Old viral posts distort the picture. A single post from years ago with a huge view count lifts the median of a channel that has since faded. The maxPostAgeDays input limits the sample to recent posts, while lastPostAt and daysSinceLastPost still report the real latest post.
  • Not every username is a channel. Users, bots, private groups and channels with the preview disabled do not answer the way a channel does. They come back as a row with found: false and a reason, instead of being silently dropped or filled with zeros.
  • Layout changes. If Telegram changes the page so that posts exist but the header or dates cannot be read, the Actor fails the run with an explicit message rather than returning empty metrics.

Step by step

The Actor runs on plain HTTP requests, so a run of a dozen channels finishes in seconds. The input takes usernames, @names or links:


{
  "channels": ["telegram", "@durov", "https://t.me/bloomberg_ru", "rian_ru"],
  "postsPerChannel": 40,
  "maxPostAgeDays": 30,
  "minSubscribers": 0,
  "maxSubscribers": 0,
  "maxItems": 100
}

From Python with apify-client:


from apify_client import ApifyClient

client = ApifyClient("YOUR_APIFY_TOKEN")
run = client.actor("datagrit/telegram-channel-growth-analytics").call(run_input={
    "channels": ["telegram", "durov", "bloomberg_ru", "rian_ru"],
    "postsPerChannel": 40,
    "maxPostAgeDays": 30,
})
rows = list(client.dataset(run["defaultDatasetId"]).iterate_items())

With cURL, in one synchronous call:


curl -X POST "https://api.apify.com/v2/acts/datagrit~telegram-channel-growth-analytics/run-sync-get-dataset-items?token=YOUR_APIFY_TOKEN" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"channels":["telegram","durov","bloomberg_ru"],"postsPerChannel":40}'

Telegram returns about 20 posts per request, so 40 posts means two requests per channel. The Actor pauses between pages and retries on throttling, honouring Retry-After.

What you get

One flat row per channel. The fields most people use:

There are more: averageViews, maxViews, mediaPostsSharePct, linkPostsSharePct, medianViewsChangePct, subscribersChangePerDay and the channel's all-time counters for photos, videos and links.

How growth tracking works

The Actor stores the subscriber count and median views of each delivered channel in a named store on your account. The first run for a channel has no earlier value, so isFirstObservation is true and the growth fields are null. From the second run, subscribersChange, subscribersChangePct, subscribersChangePerDay and medianViewsChangePct are filled, and observations counts how many runs the comparison rests on. Channels skipped by a filter or unreadable in a run are not recorded, so a failed read never overwrites a good baseline.

This means the growth data is only as good as your schedule. A daily or weekly Apify schedule with the same channel list is the intended use.

Recipes

Rank a shortlist before buying ads. Load the rows into pandas and sort by view rate, then drop channels that stopped posting:


import pandas as pd

df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
df = df[df["found"] & (df["daysSinceLastPost"] < 7)]
print(df.sort_values("viewRatePct", ascending=False)[["channel", "subscribers", "medianViews", "viewRatePct", "reactionsPer1kViews"]])

Weekly growth report into a sheet. Schedule the Actor weekly with the same channels and append channel, subscribers, subscribersChange and medianViewsChangePct to a spreadsheet through the Apify integrations. A channel whose subscribers grow while its median views fall is gaining followers who do not read it.

Find mid-size channels. Set minSubscribers to 10000 and maxSubscribers to 200000 on a long list of candidates. Channels outside the range are skipped before they are billed or recorded.

Check where a channel gets its content. Sort by forwardedSharePct and read topForwardSources. A channel that forwards most of its posts from two sources is a distribution channel for them, which matters for both partnerships and brand safety.

Limitations

  • Only channels with a public preview. Private channels and channels whose owner disabled the web preview cannot be read without logging in, and the Actor never does.
  • Subscriber counts are rounded for channels above roughly ten thousand, so small day-to-day changes are not visible there.
  • A sample, not a census. Metrics describe the sampled posts (default 40, up to 400), not the whole history. The preview shows no demographics, audience geography or private statistics that only channel admins see.
  • View counts move. Run on the same schedule if you compare channels, so that posts are of similar age.
  • Reactions can be absent. If a channel disables reactions, medianReactions and reactionsPer1kViews are null, not zero.

The Actor reads only the public page that Telegram serves to anyone. If you use the results for outreach, data protection and anti-spam rules still apply to you.

Wrapping up

Reach, cadence and growth per channel in one row is usually what a channel shortlist needs. Pricing is pay per analysed channel, the Actor is on Apify, and the full field list is in the documentation.
